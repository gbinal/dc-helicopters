### Summary

Part of the HMX-1 squadron, these distinctive tiltrotor aircraft serve as VIP transport and support missions. The V-22 Osprey combines the vertical takeoff and landing capabilities of a helicopter with the speed and range of a fixed-wing aircraft.

### Details

The V-22 Ospreys operated by HMX-1 are unique aircraft in the DC skies. Unlike traditional helicopters, they are tiltrotor aircraft that can take off and land vertically like a helicopter but fly like an airplane by rotating their engines forward. This gives them significantly greater speed and range compared to conventional helicopters.

While the VH-3 Sea Kings and VH-60 Black Hawks are the traditional Marine One aircraft, the V-22 Ospreys serve an important support role for presidential missions. They are primarily used to transport Secret Service personnel, White House staff, and presidential support equipment ahead of the President's arrival at destinations.

The Ospreys are painted in the same green and white "White Top" livery as the other HMX-1 aircraft. Their distinctive appearance - with large rotors at the end of each wing that can tilt between helicopter and airplane mode - makes them easily identifiable in the air.

### Viewing Tips

You'll most likely see the V-22 Ospreys during the following scenarios:

1) **Support for Presidential Trips** - The Ospreys typically deploy ahead of the President when he travels. You may see them departing DC before or after a presidential trip, often flying in pairs or with other HMX-1 helicopters.

2) **Joint Base Bolling** - Like other HMX-1 aircraft, V-22s operate from Joint Base Anacostia-Bolling in southwest DC. When viewing from National Airport across the river, you may see these distinctive aircraft taking off or landing near the three large hangar doors.

3) **Training Flights** - When the President is away, HMX-1 uses the opportunity for training missions. You may see the V-22s conducting practice flights around the DC area.

4) **In Formation with Other HMX-1 Aircraft** - Occasionally, V-22s will fly in formation with the presidential helicopters during movements between the White House and Andrews Air Force Base, though they typically carry support personnel rather than the President.

The V-22's distinctive appearance is unmistakable: the large tilting rotors at the wingtips, the wider wingspan compared to a helicopter, and the characteristic sound (different from a traditional helicopter) make them easy to identify even from a distance.

Nighthawk (when operating as part of HMX-1)
